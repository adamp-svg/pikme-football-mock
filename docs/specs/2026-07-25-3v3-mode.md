## 0. TL;DR

| Decision | Value | Why (one line) |
|---|---|---|
| **Pitch** | **2300 × 1300** (+15% W, +18% H) | Largest pitch where a full kick still covers **56% of half-court**; at 2500+ the ball starts living in midfield (§2.4) |
| **Goal** | width **380**, depth **70** (unchanged) | 3 defenders can wall a 300 mouth; 380 is the knee of the measured goal-width curve (§2.5) |
| **Penalty area** | **740 × 420** | Ratio-preserving (56.9% of H, 18.3% of W) |
| **Post radius** | **9** (unchanged) | Woodwork thickness is body-scaled, not pitch-scaled |
| **Team size** | 3 + 3 = **6 players** | Wire already carries 8 slots — **proven**, §3.1 |
| **Match rules** | **first-to-3, 180 s cap** | Measured p90 time-to-3 = 122 s; a 180 s cap gives exactly the same clock-headroom ratio 2v2 has (§5) |
| **Camera** | `VIEW_W` **1410** world-px (0.61·W), sprites at **0.86×** | Reproduces 2v2's exact 61%×51% pitch framing (§4) |
| **Riskiest change** | **Making `FIELD` per-match** — 133 call sites across 6 modules; `sim.js`/`bot-ai.js`/`client.js` all derive `GOAL_TOP`/`PENALTY_TOP` **at module load** (§3) | |
| **NOT risky** | **The binary wire.** It is size-agnostic (§3.1) | |

> ⚠️ **Read §2.0 before accepting the pitch number.** Rocket League and Brawl Stars both run 1v1, 2v2
> **and 3v3 on one identical arena**, and our current 2v2 pitch is already *larger* than Brawl Stars'
> 3v3 pitch in body-diameters. "Don't grow the pitch at all" is a defensible answer that removes the
> entire §3 refactor; it is costed as **option Z′** in §2.9. This spec still recommends 2300×1300, for
> three specific reasons given in §2.0, but 2300×1300 is the **upper bound** of what the evidence
> supports, and §9 step 5 is deliberately the cheap moment to change it.

**The single most important finding:** the wire is *not* the problem. `shared/wire.js` packs absolute
coordinates as i16-tenths (range ±3276.7 px) with a **u8 slot mask = 8 slots**. A 6-player match on a
2300×1300 pitch round-trips **byte-exact, zero code change** (proven in §3.1). The real risk is the
*module-load-time* geometry constants scattered through `sim.js`, `bot-ai.js` and `client.js`.

## 2. Field geometry

### 2.0 ⚠️ First, the uncomfortable finding: the comparables do NOT scale their arena

Before any of the derivation below, the honest headline from the competitive research (full data +
sources in Appendix B):

> **Rocket League and Brawl Stars both use one identical arena for 1v1, 2v2 AND 3v3.**
> Rocket League: Duel, Doubles, Standard and 4v4 Chaos all play on the same 10,240 × 8,192 uu Soccar
> field, same 34 boost pads. Brawl Stars: measured directly off the official map renders, **every**
> 1v1 / 2v2 / 3v3 mode — Brawl Ball, Brawl Ball 2v2, Gem Grab, Bounty, Duels — is **21 × 33 tiles**,
> with zero per-map variation. The footprint only jumps (to 60 × 60) at **5v5 / 10 players**.

And the comparison that should make anyone pause:

| | Long axis | Short axis | Body-areas per player |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Brawl Ball 3v3** (brawler = 1 tile) | **33 bodies** | **21 bodies** | **115** |
| **Our current 2v2** (2000×1100, body 52.5) | **38.1 bodies** | **21.0 bodies** | 200 |
| Our 3v3 **on the current pitch** | 38.1 | 21.0 | **133** |
| Our 3v3 on the proposed 2300×1300 | 43.8 | 24.8 | 181 |

**Our existing 2v2 pitch is already 15% longer than Brawl Stars' 3v3 pitch and exactly as wide.** Running
3v3 on it unchanged would still give each player **more** room than Brawl Ball does (133 vs 115
body-areas). And the pace harness agrees — see §2.4: 3v3 on the *current* pitch measures **21.2 s/goal**,
only **1.16×** the 2v2 baseline, versus **1.37×** on the proposed pitch.

So a legitimate design answer to this whole brief is **"don't grow the pitch at all."** That option is
costed and kept alive in §2.9 — and it is *dramatically* cheaper, because if 3v3 geometry is identical
to 2v2 geometry, **the entire §3 refactor disappears**.

**Why this spec still recommends growing it, to 2300×1300:**

1. **Shot-range ratio lands dead on the comparable.** Brawl Stars' longest-range brawler covers **30% of
   the map's long axis / 48% of the short axis**, and that is the documented ceiling for a game that
   wants passing play rather than shooting-gallery play. Our full kick at 2300×1300 = **28.1% / 49.8%** —
   within a point of Brawl Stars on both axes. On the *current* pitch a 3v3 full kick is 32.4% / 58.8%,
   i.e. relatively longer than Brawl Stars' best sniper, which with six players on the pitch tips toward
   long-ball spam over build-up.
2. **The goalmouth genuinely breaks at 3 defenders.** This is the argument the comparables can't settle
   for us, because neither has our keeper-catch mechanic. Two bodies on a 300-wide mouth leave
   **1.05 ball-diameters** of gap (§2.5). Something has to give — either the pitch grows or the goal
   does. If the pitch stays, `GOAL.width` **still** has to change per mode, which means the §3 machinery
   is needed anyway and most of the saving in §2.9 evaporates.
3. **The product promise is already written.** `index.html:243` ships the locked card as
   *"כדורגל · 3 נגד 3 — **מגרש גדול** · יותר טירוף"*. A 3v3 on the identical pitch is a different product.

**The one place the research argues against us, and it should be logged, not hidden:** the published
traversal-budget rules of thumb cluster at **8–14 s to cross the map** (Brawl Ball is 13.8 s; the
multiplayer-level-design rule of thumb is "~20 s unobstructed"; War Robots' designers flag their 40 s
large maps as a fatigue problem). Our 2v2 is **14.1 s** — already at the top of the band. The proposed
3v3 is **16.2 s**, over it. Two mitigations, both real:

- **Our ball:player speed ratio is 9.8:1** (full kick 1400 px/s vs run 142 px/s). Rocket League's is
  2.6:1 and its field is famously "big but fast". Traversal in this game is a **kick chain**, not a run:
  three full kicks cross 2300 px in ~6 s. The 16.2 s run figure describes a player with no ball, which
  is the least common way anyone crosses this pitch.
- The measured midfield-stall number (§2.4) is the direct test of "does it feel like a slog", and at
  2300×1300 it reads **33.7%** — up from 25.7% but nowhere near the 48% seen at 2800 wide.

**Net:** 2300×1300 is defensible and lands on the comparable's shot-range ratio, but it is the **upper
bound** of what the evidence supports, not the safe middle. §2.9 defines the fallback ladder and §9
step 5 is the gate where you decide with the mode actually in your hands.

### 2.1 The constraint set

| # | Constraint | Formula | Bound |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1** | A full kick must still be a *shot*, not a *pass*. Keep the shootable band ≥ 28% of pitch length (2v2 = 32.4%). | `647 / W ≥ 0.28` | **W ≤ 2310** |
| **C2** | 6 bodies need room. Don't exceed 2v2 density by more than ~10%. | `W·H / 6 ≥ 0.90 × 550k` | **W·H ≥ 2.97 M** |
| **C3** | On a landscape phone the *vertical* axis is the scarce one. Keep ≥ 47% of H visible at an acceptable sprite size. | see §4 | **H ≤ ~1350** |
| **C4** | Aspect must stay near 16:9–1.85 so the cached stadium background doesn't blow up. | | **1.7 ≤ W/H ≤ 1.9** |
| **C5** | Max coordinate must stay inside the wire's i16-tenths range. | `W + GOAL.depth < 3276.7` | **W ≤ 3200** (not binding) |

C1 and C2 nearly collide. C1 caps W at 2310; C2 then forces H ≥ 1286.

### 2.2 The answer

### 2.3 Why the pitch grows *less* than the roster

Roster grows 1.5× (4→6). Pitch area grows only **1.36×**. That is deliberate, and it is what every
comparable game does — the shot range does **not** scale with team size, so scaling area linearly with
players would silently nerf every offensive tool in the game by 22%. Instead:

- **Pitch length** grows the least (+15%) because it is what the kick has to cover.
- **Pitch width** grows the most (+18%) because that is what extra bodies actually need — lateral
  passing lanes, not more length.
- **Nothing body-relative changes.** `BOMB.radius` 168, `BUILT_WALL.len` 176, `BOMB_LOB_RANGE` 250,
  `BULLET_FULL_DIST` 300, all bullet/bomb/wall tuning: **unchanged**. This is the load-bearing principle
  of the whole spec — *scale the pitch, never the mechanics* — and it means `docs/MECHANICS.md` stays
  valid verbatim for 3v3.

### 2.4 Empirical proof: bigger pitch → slower match (and where it breaks)

`node scratch/pace.mjs` (Appendix A.2) mutates the exported `FIELD`/`GOAL`/`PENALTY` objects **before**
importing `sim.js` and `bot-ai.js`, so their module-load-time derived constants pick up the candidate
geometry. Then it runs bot-vs-bot matches at skill 0.6, 6 runs × 180 s each, fixed seeds.

| Config | Goals/min | **sec/goal** | vs 2v2 | Ball in middle third | Possession |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2v2 @ 2000×1100 (baseline)** | 3.28 | **18.3 s** | 1.00× | 25.7% | 58.0% |
| 3v3 @ 2000×1100 (no scale-up) | 2.83 | 21.2 s | 1.16× | 30.2% | 56.3% |
| 3v3 @ 2150×1200, g340 | 2.39 | 25.1 s | 1.37× | 32.5% | 54.9% |
| **3v3 @ 2300×1300, g380** ⬅ | **2.39** | **25.1 s** | **1.37×** | **33.7%** | 55.7% |
| 3v3 @ 2500×1400, g400 | 2.11 | 28.4 s | 1.55× | 41.8% | 57.0% |
| 3v3 @ 2800×1550, g430 | 1.78 | **33.8 s** | **1.85×** | **48.3%** | 54.7% |

Read the **"ball in middle third"** column — that is the slog metric. It measures the fraction of the
match the ball spends between 35% and 65% of pitch length, i.e. going nowhere. It climbs from 26% (2v2)
to 34% at our candidate, then to **48% at 2800 wide**: at that size the ball is in no-man's-land for
half the match. **2500 is the outer limit; 2800 is a slog. 2300 is safe.**

Note also that 2150×1200 and 2300×1300 measure *identically* on pace (25.1 s/goal) — the extra 150×100
is free. Take the bigger one: it buys the space the 6 bodies need without costing pace.

> ⚠️ These runs use the **current** bot AI, in which the third bot on each team is roleless (§3.4). The
> absolute goal rates will move once bot roles are fixed; the **relative** ordering across geometries —
> which is all this table is used for — will not.

### 2.5 Goal width — why 380

Two independent arguments; the geometric one is primary.

**Geometric.** A defender standing on the line blocks a band of `2 × (r_player + r_ball)` =
`2 × (26.25 + 32)` = **116.5 px**.

| Goal width | After 1 keeper | After 2 bodies | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 300 (today) | 183.5 px = 2.9 ball-diameters | **67 px = 1.05 ball-diameters** | With 3 defenders, two bodies on the line **seal the goal** |
| 355 (pure H-proportional) | 238.5 px = 3.7 | 122 px = 1.9 | Marginal |
| **380** ⬅ | **263.5 px = 4.1** | **147 px = 2.3** | A two-man wall still leaves a real gap |
| 420 | 303.5 px = 4.7 | 187 px = 2.9 | Starts to feel like a barn door |

380 = 29.2% of H (2v2 is 27.3%) = 355 proportional + a **7% third-defender allowance**.

**Empirical.** Goal-width sweep at 2300×1300, 8 runs × 180 s:

| Goal width | 300 | 340 | **380** | 420 | 460 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| sec/goal | 25.7 | 25.7 | **24.4** | 24.8 | 24.8 |

380 is the knee; past it the curve is flat. The bot sample under-measures goal-width sensitivity (bots
are mediocre finishers and rarely exploit a wide mouth), so treat the geometric argument as the real one
and this as confirmation that 380 isn't *worse*.

**Goal depth stays 70.** It is a body-sized net pocket (2.7 player-radii deep — enough to dribble in and
score, per `clampBallCarryXY`). It has nothing to do with pitch size. Same for `POST_R = 9`.

### 2.6 Penalty area

`PENALTY = { width: 740, depth: 420 }`.

- 740 / 1300 = **56.9%** of H (2v2: 56.4%) — ratio-preserving.
- 420 / 2300 = **18.3%** of W (2v2: 18.0%) — ratio-preserving.
- 740 / 380 = 1.95 × goal width (2v2: 2.07) — slightly tighter relative to the mouth, which is right:
  a wider mouth shouldn't drag the whole low-knockback zone out with it.
- A full kick (647 px) taken from just outside the box lands **227 px** clear of the line — the shot
  still has to be taken from open play, not from the edge of the area.

**⚠️ Balance risk this creates — the three-keeper wall.** `sim.js:878` gives *every* defender inside
their own box the keeper catch (`inOwnPenalty(p) && tier < 2` → ball dead-stops, `stat.saves++`). With
3 defenders and a 420-deep box, all three can legally be "keepers" at once — and the 3v3 kickoff
formation (§3.3) literally spawns two of them inside the box. **Three catchers in one box makes 3v3
unscoreable below overcharge.** Mitigation, in the sim:

```js
// inOwnPenalty(p) gates the catch today. Add: only the SINGLE defender nearest their own
// goal line, among those inside the box, gets the catch. Everyone else in the box is a
// FIELD defender (rebound, tier<2) but keeps the PENALTY_KNOCKBACK_MUL / attacking-box rules.
function isKeeper(state, p) {
  if (!inOwnPenalty(p)) return false;
  const line = p.team === 'A' ? 0 : GEO.W;
  for (const id in state.players) {
    const q = state.players[id];
    if (q.team !== p.team || q.id === p.id || !inOwnPenalty(q)) continue;
    if (Math.abs(q.x - line) < Math.abs(p.x - line)) return false;
  }
  return true;
}
```

Then replace the two `inOwnPenalty(p)` calls in the kicked-ball-hits-defender block (`sim.js:878, 882`)
with `isKeeper(state, p)`. **Leave `inEnemyPenalty`/`knockMul` alone** — the attacking-box knockback rule
is unrelated and should still apply to all three attackers. This change is a **no-op at 2v2**
(two defenders in one box is already rare and one-keeper is the intent), so it can ship in step 1 of the
build order and be regression-tested against the existing 2v2 suite.

= 528 B worst case. Section counts are `u8` (cap 255 entries) and the encoder scratch buffer is 8192 B,
so this is safe with ~15× headroom. Worst-case frame ≈ 850 B (see §3.1).

### 2.9 The fallback ladder — and the cheap option, costed

Because §2.0 leaves a genuine open question, define the retreat **before** building, so step 5 is a
decision and not a re-litigation.

| Option | Pitch | Goal | Measured sec/goal | Midfield stall | Traversal | Engineering |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Z — don't scale** | 2000×1100 | 380 | **21.2 s** (1.16×) | 30.2% | 14.1 s | **§3 refactor still needed** (goal width differs) but **only for `goalW`/`penW`** — no arena file, no camera work, no `sanitizeField` change |
| **Z′ — don't scale, don't touch the goal** | 2000×1100 | 300 | 21.2 s | 30.2% | 14.1 s | **§3 refactor NOT needed at all.** 3v3 = roster + bot roles + match rules only. Roughly **one third** of this spec. Fails the goalmouth test (§2.5). |
| **Y — half step** | 2150×1200 | 340 | **25.1 s** (1.37×) | 32.5% | 15.1 s | Same as X |
| **X — recommended** | **2300×1300** | **380** | **25.1 s** (1.37×) | 33.7% | 16.2 s | Full spec |
| **W — too big** | 2500×1400 | 400 | 28.4 s (1.55×) | 41.8% | 17.6 s | ❌ do not |

Note that **Y and X measure identically on pace** (25.1 s/goal) — the 150×100 px between them is free
on pacing and buys space. If step-5 playtest says "too big", the *first* retreat is **Y**, not W→X→Z.

**Retreat cost after step 5 is one line.** `GEO_3V3` is a single object literal; `BIG_FIELD` positions
are a scaled table (§2.8) that regenerates in seconds. Nothing downstream of step 5 hard-codes 2300 or
1300. **This is the main reason the build order puts a playable 3v3 at step 5 of 9** — so the expensive
decision is made with the mode on a phone, not on a spreadsheet.

**If you want option Z′ (the cheap one):** ship steps 3, 6, 7 and the roster half of 9. Skip 1, 2, 4, 5,
8 entirely. You get 3v3 in roughly a third of the work, at Brawl-Stars-standard density, and you keep
the whole big-field spec on the shelf. The cost is the goalmouth (§2.5) and the "מגרש גדול" copy.

## 3. How field size becomes per-mode — the highest-risk work

### 3.1 First: the wire is NOT the risk. Proven.

**Claim under test:** *"wire coordinate packing assumes 2000×1100."*
**Result: false.** `shared/wire.js` packs positions as `i16(round(v * 10))` — absolute tenths, range
**−3276.8 … +3276.7 px**. There is no field-relative quantisation anywhere in the codec. The slot mask is
a `u8` and the encode/decode loops run `k < 8`, so **8 slots already**.

`node scratch/wire6.mjs` (Appendix A.3) round-trips a synthetic 6-player, 3v3-sized frame through the
real `encodeKeyframe`/`decodeSnapshot`:

```
encoded bytes for 6 players: 139
decoded players: 6
max position error (px): 0.000          <- byte-exact at x = 2343, y = 1300
ball owner round-trip: p5   ball.x: 2295
elapsed round-trip: 254 (u8 cap = 255)
encoded bytes for 4 players: 105  -> delta/frame: 34
egress delta @60Hz per client: 1.99 KB/s
total egress 6 players @60Hz x6 clients: 48.9 KB/s
8-slot ceiling: decoded 8 players, 173 bytes
9 players -> decoded 8   (SILENT DROP: mask is u8)
```

**Conclusions:**

1. **No wire change is needed for 3v3.** Not one byte.
2. Egress cost of the two extra players is **+2.0 KB/s per client**; a full 6-human room is **48.9 KB/s**
   total server egress at 60 Hz. Negligible.
3. **Hard ceiling: 8 players.** A 9th is *silently dropped* (mask is `u8`, loops are `k<8`). Anything
   beyond 4v4 needs a wire version bump. Worth a one-line guard: `if (slotIds.length > 8) throw`.
4. **Second hard ceiling: `elapsed` is a `u8`** (`wire.js:41`, `u8(Math.min(255, s.elapsed|0))`). Any
   mode duration **> 255 s** silently freezes the HUD clock at 255. Our 180 s is fine; **do not exceed
   255 s** for any future mode without changing this byte.
5. Geometry itself is *not* snapshot data — it is a per-match constant, so it travels in `matchStart`
   exactly like `chars` and `settings` (§3.2).

Worst-case 3v3 frame budget: 15 (header) + 6×17 (players) + ~18×6 (bullets) + 48×11 (wall blocks,
`MAX_BUILT_WALLS=12`) + 6×8 (bombs) + blasts/impacts ≈ **850 B**, against an 8192 B scratch buffer.

### 3.2 The mechanism: one mutable `GEO` record, swapped per step

Three candidate designs were considered:

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **A.** Thread `state.field` through every function signature | ❌ ~133 call sites over `sim.js` (38), `bot-ai.js` (27), `client.js` (54), `training.js` (14). Enormous diff, guaranteed merge war with the agents currently in `sim.js`. |
| **B.** Mutate the exported `FIELD` object at match setup | ❌ **Non-starter server-side.** One Node process runs *many* rooms. A 3v3 room would clobber a concurrent 2v2 room's geometry. (This is exactly what the *harnesses* do — safe only because a harness is one config per process.) |
| **C.** A module-level `GEO` record that the sim points at the active match's geometry for the duration of one tick | ✅ Tiny diff, and correct because the server steps rooms **one at a time, synchronously**. |

**Take C.** New module:

```js
// shared/geometry.js  — the geometry of the sim step currently running.
export function makeGeo(g) {
  const o = { W: g.W, H: g.H, goalW: g.goalW, goalD: g.goalD, penW: g.penW, penD: g.penD,
              postR: g.postR ?? 9, visionRange: g.visionRange ?? 620, ballVision: g.ballVision ?? 950,
              maxBuiltWalls: g.maxBuiltWalls ?? 8 };
  o.goalTop = (o.H - o.goalW) / 2;  o.goalBottom = (o.H + o.goalW) / 2;
  o.penTop  = (o.H - o.penW)  / 2;  o.penBottom  = (o.H + o.penW)  / 2;
  o.cx = o.W / 2; o.cy = o.H / 2;
  return o;
}
export const GEO_2V2 = makeGeo({ W:2000, H:1100, goalW:300, goalD:70, penW:620, penD:360 });
export const GEO_3V3 = makeGeo({ W:2300, H:1300, goalW:380, goalD:70, penW:740, penD:420,
                                 visionRange:660, ballVision:1090, maxBuiltWalls:12 });

// The LIVE record. Fields are OVERWRITTEN in place (never rebound) so importers can
// `import { GEO }` once and always read current values — no ESM live-binding subtleties,
// no stale destructuring at module load.
export const GEO = { ...GEO_2V2 };
export function useGeo(g) { Object.assign(GEO, g); }
```

Why an assigned-in-place object rather than a re-bound `let` export: a `let` export *does* give live
bindings in ESM, but any importer that writes `const { W } = GEO` or caches `GEO.W` at module load would
silently read stale data. A mutable record fails loudly instead (`GEO.W` is always current), and it costs
nothing.

**Swap points** (every entry that reads geometry outside a tick must call `useGeo` first):

| Call site | Add |
|---|---|
| `sim.js` `step(state, inputs, dt)` — **first line** | `useGeo(state.geo \|\| GEO_2V2)` |
| `sim.js` `createState(geo)` | `state.geo = geo \|\| GEO_2V2` and `useGeo(state.geo)` |
| `sim.js` `addPlayer` / `attachBall` / `repositionKickoff` / `setField` / `seedFieldWalls` | `useGeo(state.geo)` at entry (these run outside `step`) |
| `bot-ai.js` `computeBotInputs(state, mem, dt)` — first line | `useGeo(state.geo \|\| GEO_2V2)` |
| `server.js` `snapshot(room)` / `sanitizeField(field, geo)` | pass the room's geo explicitly |
| `client.js` `enterMatch(msg)` | `useGeo(makeGeo(msg.field))` — once per match, the client only ever runs one |

`state.geo` defaults to `GEO_2V2` everywhere, so **every existing test that calls `createState()` with no
argument keeps passing untouched** (§8).

## 4. Camera + readability

### 4.1 The bug that a bigger pitch exposes

```js
// client.js:4058
scale = CAM_ZOOM * wbW / FIELD.W;
```

**Sprite size is coupled to pitch width.** Making the pitch 15% wider makes every player, the ball and
every wall render **13% smaller** as a side effect. That is not a design choice, it is an accident of the
formula — and it must be fixed before the geometry lands, or 3v3 will read as "everything shrank".

**Fix (do this in step 1, it is a no-op at 2v2):**

```js
const VIEW_W_2V2 = 2000 / 1.65;   // = 1212.12  — EXACTLY today's visible world width
// ...
scale = wbW / GEO.viewW;          // GEO.viewW = 1212 for 2v2, 1410 for 3v3
```

Now `scale` is driven by *how much world you want on screen*, which is the thing that actually matters,
and pitch size can change freely.

### 4.2 Picking `VIEW_W` for 3v3

Today's framing, measured on an iPhone 14 landscape (844×390 CSS, dpr capped 2, `ART_PX = 3.25`
→ `wbW = 520`, `scale = 0.429`):

- Visible world = **1212 × 560 px** = **61% of W × 51% of H**.
- Player renders **36.6 CSS px** across.

To reproduce that framing at 2300×1300: `VIEW_W = 0.61 × 2300 = 1403`. Round to **1410** — and note that
`0.61 × 2300 / 2.164 = 652 = 50.2%` of 1300, so the *same* number lands both axes within a point. That
is a coincidence of the 1.77 aspect being close to the phone's, and it is a good reason to keep the
aspect where it is.

`node scratch/cam.mjs` (Appendix A.4) reproduces `updateCamera()` exactly — including the goal-lead pan
and the `CAM_BACK`/`CAM_BAND` terrace clamps — and measures how often things are off-screen over
180 s of live bot play:

| Config | View | % of W × H | Sprite | **Ball off-screen** | Teammate off-screen | All teammates off |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2v2 today** | 1212×560 | 61% × 51% | 1.00× | **13.9%** | 55.8% | 55.8% |
| 3v3, same zoom | 1212×560 | 53% × 43% | 1.00× | **37.8%** | 68.6% | 55.1% |
| 3v3, `VIEW_W` 1330 | 1330×615 | 58% × 47% | 0.91× | 38.7% | 54.0% | 31.5% |
| **3v3, `VIEW_W` 1410** ⬅ | 1410×652 | **61% × 50%** | **0.86×** | **26.5%** | 46.2% | 36.6% |
| 3v3, `VIEW_W` 1600 | 1600×739 | 70% × 57% | 0.76× | 15.2% | 35.4% | 20.6% |

**Recommendation: `VIEW_W = 1410`, i.e. `viewW: 0.61 × W`, sprites at 0.86×.**

- Player diameter **36.6 → 31.5 CSS px**; ball **38 → 33 CSS px**. Both comfortably readable — a
  fingertip target is ~44 CSS px, and these are read, not tapped.
- Pitch framing is *identical* to 2v2 in both axes. Nothing about the camera "feels different".
- **Background cache actually gets cheaper**: `bgCanvas` goes 1107×778 (0.86 Mpx, 3.3 MB) →
  1063×743 (0.79 Mpx, 3.0 MB), because `scale` drops faster than the pitch grows. No memory regression
  on the WebView, which matters given `OPTIMIZATION_TODO.md`.
- **Keep `ART_PX = 3.25`.** Dropping it to 3.0 would restore sprite pixel-detail at 0.86× zoom, but it
  raises the world-buffer resolution 17% and therefore the per-frame fill cost by the same. Not worth it;
  accept slightly chunkier players.

### 4.3 The residual — ball off-screen 26.5% vs 13.9%

`VIEW_W = 1410` recovers most, but not all, of the readability. The remaining gap is **inherent**: the
pitch is bigger, so the ball is genuinely further away more often. `VIEW_W = 1600` would fix it
numerically but costs 24% of sprite size, which is a worse trade on a phone. Close the gap with UI, not
zoom:

**Recommended: off-screen edge indicators (do this).**
- A small chevron pinned to the viewport edge on the bearing to the **ball**, always, whenever the ball
  is outside the view. Colour = possessing team (`TEAM.A.color` / `TEAM.B.color`, grey when loose).
- A smaller chevron per off-screen **teammate**, carrying their hero colour, fading in only when they're
  in the attacking half (a defender behind you is noise).
- Distance is encoded as chevron **size**, not a number: `size = lerp(14, 6, dist / diag)` art px.
- Cost: ~40 lines in the world-draw pass, no new state, no wire change, zero perf impact.

**Optional, if playtest still says "I'm lost": a corner minimap.**
- 3v3 only, top-inner corner (RTL-aware), `W/12 × H/12` = 192×108 art px, 55% alpha.
- Dots only: 6 players (team colour, mine ringed), ball (white), the goal mouths. No walls, no bushes —
  a minimap that shows cover defeats the fog-of-war/bush stealth system (`BUSH_REVEAL_DIST`,
  `canSeeEnemy`) which is a core mechanic. **The minimap must respect fog: only draw enemies the local
  player can actually see**, or 3v3 quietly deletes ambushes.
- That constraint is why the minimap is *second* choice: edge chevrons for the ball (always known) and
  teammates (always known) leak nothing, and are cheaper.

**Rejected: follow-cam on the ball.** The camera already leads toward the goal (`updateCamera`, the
`LEAD_ZONE`/`LEAD_MAX` block). Retargeting to the ball would break the twin-stick aim mapping
(`screenToWorld`) and make your own player drift off-centre, which is much worse than losing sight of
the ball.

**Rejected: dynamic zoom-to-fit.** Zoom that changes with player spread makes a pixel-art game shimmer
(`ART_PX` snapping is integer-based) and would force a `renderBackground()` rebuild on every zoom step.

### 5.2 The decision: first-to-3, 180 s cap

**3v3 stays first-to-N, not timed.** Reason: `goalsToWin` is what makes the mode feel like a *match* with
a win moment ("גול! → ניצחון!", `sim.js:581`); the timed format already exists as goal-brawl and is
being retired to an event slot per `mode-polish.md §6`. Adding a second timed mode would duplicate it.

**Why N = 3 and not 4 or 5.** The design invariant in 2v2 is that *goals decide the match and the clock
is a safety net*, not the reverse. Quantify it as **clock headroom = cap ÷ p90(time-to-N)**:

| Format | p90 time-to-N | Cap | Headroom | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **2v2, first-to-3, 120 s** (today) | 82 s | 120 s | **1.46×** | the reference |
| 3v3, first-to-3, **120 s** | 122 s | 120 s | 0.98× | ❌ 10% of matches die on the clock |
| **3v3, first-to-3, 180 s** ⬅ | 122 s | 180 s | **1.48×** | ✅ matches 2v2 exactly |
| 3v3, first-to-4, 180 s | 173 s | 180 s | 1.04× | ❌ same failure as above |
| 3v3, first-to-4, 250 s | 173 s | 250 s | 1.45× | ❌ 250 s is too long for a mobile session — and it's over the wire's 255 s `elapsed` byte |

`first-to-3 / 180 s` is the only combination that preserves the 2v2 invariant inside a sane match length.

**Resulting session length.** Median 3v3 = 84 s of play + `INTRO_PROMO` 4.6 s + 3 × `GOAL_RESET` 5 s
= **~104 s** wall-clock, vs 2v2's ~66 s. **1.6× longer** — right for the "big mode", still well under
two minutes. p90 is 180 + 4.6 + ~25 = ~210 s.

**Note the 5-goal option is off the table on data.** `lobby-game-picker.md:408/426` shows the 3v3 card
copy as `ראשון ל-5`. First-to-5 has a median of **165 s** and a p90 of **233 s** — a 4-minute median
session with the resets. **That copy needs to change to `ראשון ל-3 · עד 3 דק'`** (§7.1).

### 5.3 Implementation

`MATCH_DURATION` and `GOALS_TO_WIN` are module constants read directly by `sim.js:554/581` *and*
imported by `client.js:6` for the HUD clock. Both must become per-match:

```js
// sim.js:554
if (!state.noClock && state.phase === 'playing' && state.elapsed >= (state.duration || MATCH_DURATION))
// createState: state.duration = geo.duration || MATCH_DURATION
// server startMatch: room.state.duration = MODE.duration
// matchStart payload: duration, goalsToWin  (client HUD reads these instead of the constants)
```

`state.goalsToWin` already exists and is already set per-room (`server.js:550`) — only `duration` is new.

**⚠️ `elapsed` is a `u8` on the wire.** 180 is fine, 255 is the hard ceiling (§3.1).

### 6.4 ⚠️ 3v3 must NOT be a third public queue

This spec **adopts** `mode-roster-research.md §5.4` and `mode-polish.md §6`:

> *"3v3 goes in the rotating slot, not as a third permanent card. It needs 6 humans, which at our λ is ~0."*

Concretely:

- **No `publicRoom3v3` global.** Do not add a third standing pool. The matchmaking window is
  `COUNTDOWN_TIME = 5 s` (`server.js:71`); splitting an already-thin population three ways gives a
  6-slot room that fills with 5 bots.
- **3v3 ships party-first**: available in the party/private picker (`setGameMode`) and to friend
  challenges, where the humans arrive together. That is who the mode is *for*.
- **Public exposure is via the rotating "מצב היום" slot** — the existing `publicRoomBrawl` pool
  re-pointed at whichever format is featured, per `mode-polish.md`. When 3v3 is the featured format,
  `publicRoomBrawl` is created with `modeId:'3v3'`. **One extra pool total, not two.**
- **Solo entry stays available** via `startBotGame(member, diffLevel, '3v3')` — human + 5 bots. This is
  also the fastest way to playtest the mode during development, and needs no matchmaking at all.

## 7. Six players

### 7.1 Slots and formation

`spawnPos(team, slot)` (`sim.js:167`) hardcodes two slots. Make it formation-table driven:

```js
// sim.js — fractions of (W, H); team B mirrors x. Keyed by team size.
const FORMATION = {
  2: [[0.15, 0.36], [0.15, 0.64]],                   // unchanged — byte-identical to today
  3: [[0.18, 0.50], [0.11, 0.32], [0.11, 0.68]],     // 1 forward + 2 backs
};
function spawnPos(team, slot, geo = GEO) {
  const f = (FORMATION[geo.teamSize] || FORMATION[2])[slot] || FORMATION[2][0];
  return { x: team === 'A' ? geo.W * f[0] : geo.W * (1 - f[0]), y: geo.H * f[1] };
}
```

Absolute 3v3 spawns (team A; B mirrors about x=1150):

| Slot | Role at kickoff | Position | Distance to centre spot |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0** | **CENTRE FORWARD** — takes the kickoff | (414, 650) | **736 px** |
| 1 | BACK HIGH | (253, 416) | 928 px |
| 2 | BACK LOW | (253, 884) | 928 px |

**Why slot 0 sits at 0.18·W and not 0.15·W:** the 2v2 kickoff race is 717 px from spawn to the centre
spot. 0.18 × 2300 gives **736 px** — within 3% of it. **The kickoff sprint feels identical**, on a pitch
15% longer. That is the whole derivation.

The two backs at x=253 flank the goal mouth (which spans y 460–840 at `goalW=380`) at y 416 / 884 —
just outside the posts, which is the correct shape for a two-back line and reads instantly.

**⚠️ Kickoff possession bug this exposes.** `attachBall(state, team)` (`sim.js:210`) picks
`Object.values(state.players).find(p => p.team === team)` — *whichever team member was inserted first*.
In 2v2 both spawns are symmetric so it never mattered. In 3v3 an insertion-order accident would hand the
kickoff to a **back**, 928 px from the ball. Fix explicitly (and it improves 2v2 determinism too):

```js
const holder = Object.values(state.players).filter(p => p.team === team)
  .sort((a, b) => (a.slot ?? 9) - (b.slot ?? 9))[0];   // slot 0 = the designated kickoff taker
```

### 7.2 Keeper rules

There is **no explicit keeper** in this game and there should not be one — the keeper is emergent, via
`inOwnPenalty()`. The only change 3v3 needs is the **one-keeper-per-team** rule in §2.6, because a
420-deep box can hold three catchers. Everything else (`enemyGoalTarget`'s nearest-defender heuristic at
`sim.js:111-120`, `PENALTY_KNOCKBACK_MUL`, `KEEPER_BREAK_ROLL`) works unchanged with 3 players.

### 7.3 Wire slot count

**No change.** Proven in §3.1: 8 slots exist, 6 round-trip byte-exact, +2.0 KB/s per client.
Add the `>8` throw as a guard.

### 7.4 Bot roles — the third bot is currently invisible

**Diagnosis, measured.** `node scratch/roles3.mjs` (Appendix A.6):

```
team A bots: A0, A1, A2
assignRoles -> { onBall: 'A0', support: 'A1' }
A2 has a role? false
mean pairwise distance between the 3 team-A bots: 420 px
mean distance between the ROLELESS bot and the SUPPORT bot: 321 px   <- they shadow each other
(player diameter is 53 px)
```

`assignRoles` (`bot-ai.js:438`) handles `bots.length === 0`, `=== 1`, and implicitly `=== 2`. With three,
`support = bots.find(p => p.id !== onBall)` returns the **first** non-onBall bot and the third gets **no
role at all**. In `decideBot`, `isOnBall = role.onBall === p.id` is false for it, so it silently runs the
**support** branch — two bots executing identical support tactics, trailing each other at **321 px**
(6 body-diameters) for the whole match. The team plays 2.5v3.

**Fix: a third role, `cover`.**

```js
// bot-ai.js — roles for a team of N bots.
//   onBall  : press the carrier / chase the loose ball / carry            (existing)
//   support : attack outlet when attacking, cover shadow when defending   (existing)
//   cover   : NEW — the deepest bot. Holds the line between the ball and
//             OUR goal, never crosses halfway while we are in 'defense',
//             becomes the outlet-behind on 'attack'.
export function assignRoles(state, team, mem, dt) {
  const belief = updateBelief(state, team, mem);
  const bots = Object.values(state.players).filter(p => p.team === team);
  if (!bots.length) { mem.teams[team] = null; return null; }
  const focus = ...;                                   // unchanged
  const onBall = /* unchanged nearest-to-focus + SWITCH_MARGIN/MIN_HOLD hysteresis */;
  const rest = bots.filter(p => p.id !== onBall);
  const ogX = ownGoalX(team);
  // Among the remaining bots the DEEPEST (nearest our own goal) covers; the other supports.
  rest.sort((a, b) => Math.abs(a.x - ogX) - Math.abs(b.x - ogX));
  const cover   = rest.length >= 2 ? rest[0].id : null;   // null at 2v2 -> byte-identical behaviour
  const support = rest.length >= 2 ? rest[1].id : (rest[0]?.id ?? null);
  return (mem.teams[team] = { onBall, support, cover, mode: ballMode(state, team), since, belief });
}
```

Then in `decideBot`:

```js
const isOnBall  = role.onBall === p.id;
const isCover   = role.cover  === p.id;
const isSupport = !isOnBall && !isCover;
const mate = state.players[isOnBall ? role.support : role.onBall];   // unchanged for on-ball/support
```

**`cover` tactics** (a new branch, ~60 lines, mirroring the existing support branch's structure):

| Team mode | `cover` does |
|---|---|
| `defense` | Hold a point on the segment from the ball-belief to our own goal, at `0.30` of the way from the goal. Clamp `x` to our own half (`|x − ogX| ≤ 0.45·GEO.W`). Never chase the carrier — that's `onBall`'s job. Face the ball; bank ammo. |
| `loose` | Drop to the same anchor but 0.40 of the way out; this is the safety valve against a fast break (the existing `fastBreak` check at `bot-ai.js:1025` already computes exactly this condition — reuse it). |
| `attack` | Become the **outlet behind**: hold `0.35·GEO.W` behind the carrier on the carrier's `y`-side, staying goal-side. This is where the extra body actually adds value — a recycle option that the 2v2 game never had. |
| any | Prefer a bush within 200 px of the anchor (reuse the existing ambush preference at `bot-ai.js:1003`). Build walls **only** in our own third — a cover bot building at halfway is the current support bot's most annoying habit and it would now happen twice. |

**Two more small bot changes for 3v3:**

- `SWITCH_MARGIN = 120` is a 2v2 hysteresis tuned to a 2000-wide pitch. With three bots the onBall
  handoff churns more; raise to **160** when `teamSize === 3` (or scale it `120 × GEO.W / 2000` = 138 and
  round up). Cheap, and it stops the two nearest bots trading the ball role every half-second.
- The **two-bomb stack coordination** at `bot-ai.js:895-903` explicitly recruits "a NEARBY support bot".
  With a `cover` bot present it should recruit *whichever* of support/cover is nearest and already
  within the "no cross-map sprint" radius — one-line predicate change, but skipping it means the stack
  combo silently gets rarer in 3v3.

**Caveat that applies to A.2, A.4, A.5, A.6:** all bot-driven numbers were taken with the **current**
(2-role) bot AI, so the third bot on each team is roleless. Absolute rates will shift after step 6;
the comparisons *between* configurations, which is what every decision here rests on, will not.

## Appendix B — competitive reference data

Gathered this pass. Only the parts that bear on a decision above are kept; sources at the end.

### B.1 Rocket League — one arena for every team size

| Value | uu |
|---|---|
| Length (back wall to back wall) | **10,240** |
| Width (side wall to side wall) | **8,192** |
| Ceiling | 2,044 |
| Goal opening width | **1,785.5** (= **21.8% of field width**, 17.4% of length) |
| Goal height / depth | 642.8 / 880 |
| Ball radius / max ball speed | 91.25 / **6,000 uu/s** |
| Car max boosting / supersonic / unboosted | 2,300 / 2,200 / 1,410 uu/s |
| Aspect | 1.25 : 1 |

- **Duel (1v1), Doubles (2v2), Standard (3v3) and 4v4 Chaos all use the identical arena**, identical
  corner geometry, identical 34-pad boost layout. Only the venue skin changes. Psyonix deliberately
  deleted their non-standard arenas: *"We see Rocket League as a digital sport… standardization is
  important and necessary to provide a level playing field."* Design director Corey Davis added that
  differently shaped arenas *"didn't create enough strategic or tactical differences to justify"*
  invalidating players' learned ball physics.
- **Ball : player speed ratio = 2.6 : 1.** The field is 1.71 s wide at max ball speed but 4.45 s wide at
  max car speed. This is the transferable constant: an unlimited-range game balances via
  *projectile-to-player speed*, not via range caps. **Our ratio is 9.8 : 1** (kick 1400 vs run 142) —
  far more extreme, which is why our traversal-by-run figure (§2.0) overstates how big the pitch feels.
- **Area per player:** 1v1 4,193 m² · 2v2 2,097 · **3v3 1,398** · 4v4 1,048.
- **4v4 Chaos, same arena:** the two named failure modes are constant collisions/demos and **boost
  economy collapse** — "the size of the field and amount of boost pads does not increase for the 4v4
  mode." Pulled from the main casual menu Nov 2023.
- **Counter-example that matters:** when Psyonix designed an arena *for* 2v2 from scratch — **Hoops /
  Dunk House, 5,933 × 7,162 uu** — they made it **50.7% of the Soccar footprint**. Per player that is
  ~10.6 M uu², i.e. roughly the *3v3-on-Soccar* density. **A purpose-built arena for a smaller team is
  much smaller, not the same.** Read the other way: Soccar is sized for 3v3, and 1v1/2v2 just live with
  the extra room.
- **Rocket League Sideswipe** (mobile, 2D, heavily reduced arena) **caps at 2v2** — reported rationale
  is that more per side "would have been too messy visually." A direct team-size-follows-arena-size call,
  and the closest comparable to our constraint (a phone screen).

### B.2 Brawl Stars — measured, not quoted

Wiki tile counts were inaccessible, so the research agent **measured the grids directly** off the
official Brawlify/BrawlAPI map renders (checkerboard period detection), across all 1,239 maps in the API.

| Render | px | px/tile | rendered | **playable grid** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **All 3v3 modes** | 690 × 1050 | 30 | 23 × 35 | **21 × 33** |
| Showdown | 930 × 930 | 15 | 62 × 62 | 60 × 60 |
| 5v5 modes | 1860 × 1860 | 30 | 62 × 62 | 60 × 60 |

- **21 × 33 is used by:** Brawl Ball, Gem Grab, Bounty, Heist, Hot Zone, Knockout, **Duels (1v1)**, and
  **every 2v2 variant** (Brawl Ball 2v2, Gem Grab 2v2, Knockout 2v2 …). Backyard Bowl, Pinball Dreams,
  Sneaky Fields, Center Stage, Triple Dribble — all identical. **Zero per-map size variation.**
  Supercell varies obstacle density and openness, never footprint.
- **60 × 60 only appears at 5v5 / 10-player.** That is the only place the footprint jumps.
- **Scale:** 1 tile = 300 internal movement units; a "Normal" 720-speed brawler covers **2.4 tiles/s**.
  Brawler hitbox ≈ **1 tile**. The pitch is literally 21 brawlers wide × 33 long.
- **Range vs field — the number this spec leans on:** attack ranges run 2–10 tiles; the longest in the
  game is 10. So the **best sniper covers 30% of the long axis and 48% of the short axis**, and the
  median brawler ~24% / 38%. *Nothing shoots across a Brawl Ball pitch.*
- **Traversal:** 33 tiles ÷ 2.4 = **13.8 s** end-to-end; 8.8 s across. Brawl Ball match timer 2:30, so a
  full traversal is ~9% of the match.
- **Tiles per player:** Duels 1v1 346 · 2v2 173 · **3v3 115.5** · 5v5 360 (reused Showdown geometry).
- **No Supercell statement on map size vs pacing exists publicly.** The revealed preference above is the
  evidence.

### B.3 Fortnite and general heuristics

- **Fortnite BR:** ~5.5 × 5.5 km island, 100 players → **~0.3 km²/player**; PUBG Erangel 8 × 8 km →
  0.64 km²/player, i.e. Fortnite is ~2× denser and correspondingly faster.
- **Fortnite Creative unit scale:** 1 m = 100 uu, **1 grid tile = 512 uu = 5.12 m**. Large Island
  155 × 155 tiles (794 m) · **XL Island 215 × 215** (1,101 m) · Mega Island 899 × 899.
  The v16.50 patch was literally titled **"XL Islands, Large Team Support, and More"** — Epic shipped
  the bigger canvas and bigger team support in the same release. About as explicit as
  map-scale-follows-team-size gets from a publisher.
- **Small end:** Epic's official *Arenas Boxfights* rotates **3×3 / 3×5 / 4×4 / 5×5** build tiles for
  1v1 — ~15 × 15 m.
- **War Robots** (6v6) is the most explicitly quantified public case: **small maps 800 × 800 m for close
  combat, medium 1000 × 1000 for medium range, large 1200 × 1200 for long range** — map size class is
  chosen *to match the intended engagement distance*. Their own designers flag that on large maps
  players spend **~4 of 10 match minutes walking**, which they call a failure.
- **Little Big Robots** (4v4 top-down, same studio): Beacon Rush maps **1.5×** the Crystal Rush maps;
  10-player BR maps **3×**. Roughly linear in player count.
- **Published traversal rules of thumb:** "run end-to-end in ~20 s unobstructed", "spawn to the main
  firefight in ~10 s"; Bobby Ross's multiplayer level-design guide puts first contact at **15–45 s**
  from spawn (30 s short, 90 s long). **There is no published "m² per player" formula.** The closest
  general principle is that area per player is held approximately constant as player count scales —
  which is precisely what Rocket League and Brawl Stars *do not* do below 5v5.
- **Engagement-distance metrics** (Level Design Book): TF2 close ≤ 256 uu / medium ≤ 1,024 / long 2,048;
  Doom AI ranged cap 2,048. Stated principle: the design lever is **sightline length**, gated *below*
  max weapon range. Our analogue is `VISION_RANGE` (§2.7), which is why it is defined as
  "≈ half the visible screen width" and scales with the camera, not with the pitch.

### B.4 The two transferable rules, applied to this game

1. **Cap max shot range at ~30% of the long axis / ~48% of the short axis** if you want possession and
   passing rather than shooting-gallery play (Brawl Stars' measured ceiling). Our full kick at 2300×1300
   = **28.1% / 49.8%**. ✅ On the current pitch a 3v3 kick would be 32.4% / 58.8% — over on both axes.
2. **Target 8–14 s to traverse the pitch.** Brawl Ball 13.8 s; the FPS rule of thumb ~20 s; War Robots
   40 s = fatigue. Our 2v2 is 14.1 s, our proposed 3v3 is **16.2 s** — over. Mitigated but not erased by
   our 9.8:1 ball:player speed ratio (§2.0). **This is the strongest single argument for option Y or Z
   in §2.9** and should be weighted in the step-5 playtest.
